# Learning Plan: Electric Guitar (6-string) + Bass (4-string)

*Research summary, compiled 21 Oct 2026, for your review before anything is built or hosted.*

Goal: **play any song** (from tabs and by ear) and **write your own songs**, learning both instruments steadily with realistic daily practice.

Four research threads (about 200 web sources in total) covered:

1. how electric guitar is taught,
2. how bass is taught,
3. the science of practice and habits,
4. ear training, theory and songwriting.

---

## 1. Key conclusions

1. **Use one proven, ordered curriculum.** Every serious source says the same thing: beginners stall when they don't know what to practise next. The app should pick today's tasks for you.
   - Guitar course: **JustinGuitar Grades 1–3** (free). Reddit's r/Guitar FAQ recommends it as the default.
   - Bass course: **StudyBass Fundamentals plus TalkingBass videos** (both free), with **BassBuzz's** lesson order as a reference. JustinGuitar has no bass course.
2. **How you practise matters more than how long.** Duke, Simmons & Cash (2009) found that playing *accurately* during practice predicted results. Total minutes did not. Practice volume explains only about 21% of skill differences (Macnamara 2014 meta-analysis). The app should reward clean repetitions, not time.
3. **Short, frequent sessions win.** Spread-out practice beats cramming. Much of early motor learning happens during short rests. Start with 10–15 minutes a day while your fingertips toughen (2–4 weeks), and build up to 30–45 minutes split into blocks.
4. **Mix skills within a session, and review old ones on a schedule.** Rotate 2–3 items every 3–5 minutes (interleaving) instead of drilling one thing. Revisit learned items after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days.
5. **Learn theory once and use it on both instruments.** Bass strings E-A-D-G are the same notes as guitar's four lowest strings, one octave lower. Note names, intervals, keys and chord numbers carry over directly. Practise each concept on guitar as chords, then on bass as roots and arpeggios.
6. **Train your ears from day 1, in small doses (5–10 minutes).** Functional ear training (hearing a note's role in a key) is the skill that lets you work out songs by ear. Bass lines are the easiest part to hear, so playing bass speeds up learning songs on guitar too.
7. **Start creating early.** Record one 4-chord loop in week 2–3 and hum over it. Write a "bad song" every week from month 3. Write songs by copying a song and then changing it. Recording guitar and then adding your own bass line trains both instruments *and* songwriting.
8. **Avoid streak guilt.** Broken streaks make people quit (Silverman & Barasch 2023). Use a weekly goal ("5 of 7 days"), automatic freezes, and a 2-minute fallback session after a missed day. Reward milestones, not minutes.
9. **Prevent injury.** Skipping warm-ups is the factor most linked to playing injuries. Press only as hard as you need to, check in on pain, and take one rest day a week.

## 2. Weekly structure: chosen by the app from your availability

*(Your change: equal split, and no fixed days.)*

At setup the app asks **how many days a week** you want to practise, **which days**, and **how long** each session is. It then balances the two instruments **equally**:

- **Short sessions (under 40 minutes):** each session focuses on **one instrument**. The app picks whichever instrument has had **fewer minutes so far**. If you miss a day or practise on an extra one, the balance still comes out even.
- **Long sessions (40 minutes or more, once you're past the first month):** each session covers **both instruments** in two blocks with a break between. The app swaps which instrument goes first each time.
- **Studio Day:** about once every 5 sessions, starting in week 5 (after the first 4 weeks and at least 8 sessions). You record a chord progression on guitar, write a bass line over it and hum a melody, which is your songwriting practice.
- Days you didn't choose are **rest days**. You can still do a 2-minute ear game, or practise anyway.
- **First weeks:** your session length is capped at 15 minutes in weeks 1–4 and 25 minutes in weeks 5–12, so your fingertips can toughen up. After that you get your full chosen length.
- **Shared skills** (ear training, fretboard notes and theory) appear in every session and count for both instruments.
- You can change your days or session length at any time, and the schedule recalculates.

## 3. Daily session template

Every session follows this order. The minutes grow with each phase.

| Block | Weeks 1–4 (12 min) | Months 2–3 (25 min) | Months 4–12 (35–45 min, 2 blocks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Warm-up: stretches, slow chromatic runs | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| 2. Technique focus: one target, using the *goal → attempt → spot the error → adjust* loop with a tempo ladder | 4 | 7 | 10 |
| 3. Song or riff: apply today's skill, plus one older song due for review | 4 | 8 | 10 |
| ☕ Break | — | 1 | 3–5 |
| 4. Ears and fretboard: interval, note-finding or functional ear drills | 1 | 3 | 7 |
| 5. Create: riff, loop or writing your own words (object writing), recorded on your phone | — | 2 | 5 |
| 6. Log: clean BPM, what broke, tomorrow's target | 1 | 1 | 1 |

**Tempo ladder rule** (in the app): find the fastest tempo where you get 3 clean repetitions in a row. Add 4–8 BPM after a clean set. Drop back 10–20% after repeated mistakes. Play a short burst at the target speed now and then.

## 4. The curriculum (about 12 months, then ongoing)

Timelines assume about 5–6 practice days a week. They are teacher and community estimates, not guarantees. **You only move to the next phase after passing its checkpoint**, so it's fine if a phase takes longer.

### Phase 0: Setup (Week 1)
- **Both:** tuning with a tuner, posture, strap height, reading tab and chord diagrams, plugging in.
- **Guitar:** holding the pick, finding a clean amp tone (EQ knobs at 12 o'clock, gain low).
- **Bass:** plucking-hand basics (alternating index and middle fingers, rest strokes), thumb resting on the pickup ("moveable anchor").
- ✅ Checkpoint: tune both instruments alone; play open strings evenly to a metronome at 60 BPM.

### Phase 1: Foundations and calluses (Weeks 1–4) · 10–15 min/day
- **Guitar:** A, D and E chords; all-down strums; **One Minute Changes** (count how many clean chord changes you make in 60 seconds); your first one-string riff (*Seven Nation Army*); one-finger power chords.
- **Bass:** one-finger-per-fret "spider" exercise; strict i-m alternation; root notes in eighth notes; muting with both hands starting in week 2.
- **Shared:** the 12-note musical alphabet; note names on the E and A strings; matching a pitch by singing it, then finding it.
- ✅ Checkpoint: guitar, A↔D at 15+ changes a minute; bass, steady eighth notes at 80 BPM with no strings ringing by accident; one riff played on each.

### Phase 2: First songs (Months 2–3) · 20–25 min/day
- **Guitar:** Em, Am, Dm, C and G (the 8 essential open chords); the "Old Faithful" strum (D-DU-UDU); the capo; 2-finger power chords with palm muting; alternate picking on riffs.
- **Bass:** root-fifth and octave shapes; following a drum loop; rests and syncopation; reading eighth-note rhythms; full songs played along with the recording.
- **Shared:** half and whole steps; intervals (3rds, 5ths, octaves) by ear; hearing major vs minor chords; your first recorded 4-chord loop.
- **Songs:** guitar, *Horse with No Name*, *Wild Thing*, *Knockin' on Heaven's Door*, *Smoke on the Water*. Bass, *Seven Nation Army*, *Another One Bites the Dust*, *Come As You Are*, *Back in Black*.
- ✅ Checkpoint: all 8 open chords at about 30 changes a minute; one 3-minute song played start to finish on each instrument.

### Phase 3: Theory that unlocks songs (Months 4–6) · 30–35 min/day
- **Guitar:** 8th-note strumming; Cadd9/G5 chords; the "mini F"; open E minor pentatonic, then A minor pentatonic pattern 1; hammer-ons and pull-offs; sus and 7th chords; the 12-bar blues shuffle.
- **Bass:** minor/major pentatonic and major scale (one octave); major and minor arpeggios; chromatic approach notes; locking with the kick drum; the 12-bar blues.
- **Shared:** the major scale formula; **chords in a key (I ii iii IV V vi) and Nashville numbers**; common progressions (I-IV-V, I-V-vi-IV, vi-IV-I-V, I-vi-IV-V); Functional Ear Trainer levels 1–3; **your first full song** (verse and chorus).
- **Songs:** guitar, *Let It Be*, *Stand By Me*, *Blitzkrieg Bop*, *Redemption Song*. Bass, *Stand By Me*, *With or Without You*, *Yellow*, *Psycho Killer*.
- ✅ Checkpoint: improvise over a 12-bar blues on guitar; play roots, 5ths and arpeggios through I-IV-V-vi on bass; name the chord numbers in a simple song.

### Phase 4: Lead, groove and ears (Months 7–9) · 35–45 min/day
- **Guitar:** 16th-note strumming; bends (in tune, checked against the target fret) and vibrato; *Wonderwall*-style anchor fingers; licks in pentatonic pattern 1–2; dynamics.
- **Bass:** 16th notes; ghost notes; improving muting; two-beat (country) feel; dynamics and note length.
- **Shared:** recognising I-IV-V and I-V-vi-IV progressions by ear; **finding the key of a song**; **transcription starts** (work out bass lines first, using slowed-down audio); weekly "bad song"; writing a riff from the pentatonic scale.
- **Songs:** guitar, *Wonderwall*, *Smells Like Teen Spirit*, *Sweet Home Alabama*, *Wish You Were Here* intro. Bass, *Under Pressure*, *Should I Stay or Should I Go*, *Come Together*.
- ✅ Checkpoint: work out a simple pop bass line by ear; clean whole-step bends; record your own song with guitar and bass.

### Phase 5: Figure it out yourself (Months 10–12)
- **Guitar:** full E-shape and A-shape barre chords; triads on the top 3 strings; notes on the 6th and 5th strings (needed for moving barre chords); the CAGED introduction.
- **Bass:** walking bass over a blues (root, then chord or scale tones, then a chromatic approach); basic slap (optional); 7th-chord arpeggios.
- **Shared:** **transcribe one whole song a week with no tab**, then check against a tab; identify the 7 chord qualities by ear; borrowed chords (bVII, iv); write song structures (verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge).
- **Songs:** guitar, *Wonderful Tonight*, plus songs you choose and transcribe. Bass, *Money* (7/4 time), *Billie Jean*, *Superstition* (stretch goal).
- ✅ **"Graduate beginner"** checkpoint: learn most pop/rock songs from a tab in one sitting, and simpler ones by ear; a finished original song on both instruments.

### Phase 6: Year 2 and beyond (Fluency)
All 5 pentatonic patterns; the full major scale on the neck; CAGED; modes (Mixolydian and Dorian first); seventh chords and extensions; Troy Grady-style picking work if you want speed; transcribing solos and vocal melodies; songwriting challenges (FAWM in February, 50/90 in summer).

## 5. Milestone tests built into the app

| Test | Instrument | Pass mark |
|---|---|---|
| One Minute Changes | Guitar | 30 or more clean changes in 60 s (later 60) |
| Clean-tempo ladder | Both | Personal-best clean BPM per exercise, graphed over time |
| Song start to finish | Both | Play along with the recording without stopping |
| Ear quiz | Shared | 80% or more on the current level (intervals, then chord quality, then scale degrees, then progressions) |
| Note finder | Shared | Name any note on the E/A strings in under 2 s |
| Monthly "then vs now" recording | Both | Your own before/after comparison |

## 6. What the app would do (proposed features)

**Core**
- 📅 **Today screen:** the exact tasks for today (instrument, minutes, steps, links to the free lesson videos), with a timer that moves you through each block.
- 🧭 **Path / roadmap:** a visual map of the phases with checkpoints; you unlock the next phase by passing its tests.
- 🔁 **Spaced review queue:** songs and skills you've learned come back after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days. Rate them solid, shaky or lost.

**Built-in tools** (no other apps needed)
- 🥁 Metronome and simple drum-loop groove player (rock, funk, shuffle), with a tempo-ladder mode
- 🎯 Tuner (microphone-based) for guitar and bass
- 🎼 Interactive fretboard: guitar/bass toggle, note-finder game, scales, chords in a key
- 👂 Ear trainer: intervals, major/minor, scale degrees in a key, common progressions
- ⏱️ One Minute Changes timer and counter
- 📝 Songwriting pad: chord-progression builder in Nashville numbers, playback, writing-prompt generator, idea log

**Progress and motivation** (based on the research)
- Weekly goal ring ("5 of 7 days") instead of a fragile daily streak; 2 automatic freezes a week
- 2-minute "minimum day" session if you're tired or after a missed day; no guilt notifications
- BPM graphs, song library ("songs I can play"), milestone badges for real achievements
- Practice journal: what broke and tomorrow's target, which pre-fills the next session
- Pain check-in: if you report pain, the next session gets shorter

**Tech**
- One installable web app (PWA) hosted free on **GitHub Pages**. Add it to the home screen on iPhone, Android and Mac; it works offline.
- Progress is saved on each device, with Export/Import backup. Sync between devices would need a free account service.

## 8. Caveats

- **Weak or unverified evidence:**
  - Most music-practice studies are small (10–25 people).
  - The "90% of beginners quit" figure is from a Fender customer survey, not published research.
  - Sleep-consolidation gains are debated.
  - No controlled study proves metronome ladders work.
- **Estimates, not research:** timelines come from teachers and communities. Song choices are compiled from many beginner lists.
- **Not built into the app:** the app won't *listen* and grade your playing the way Yousician does. Self-recording and checkpoints take that role. A few lessons with a real teacher early on help catch posture and tension problems.
- **Song tunings:** some songs use non-standard tuning. For example, *Come As You Are* is tuned down a whole step. The app will flag these.
